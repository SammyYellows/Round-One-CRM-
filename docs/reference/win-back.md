# Ex-members: who is worth winning back, when, and how?

Research note for Round One, 11/10/2026. Written to help plan mailouts and
staff calls to people whose membership has ended. It is reference
material: no message, automation or setting changes because of it. It is
the companion to `docs/reference/sleeping-members.md`, which covers
members who still pay but have stopped coming.

It covers both halves of winning people back. Sections 1 to 7 are about
people whose membership has actually ended, reached through the Mailouts
screen (`docs/mailouts.md`) or a phone call. Section 8 is the notice
period, between someone giving notice in TeamUp and their last day: it
answers `docs/improvement-list.md` item 17 (timing, offers, tone and a
second touch) and looks at the live "Gave notice – win-back" email and
WhatsApp (item 7), with suggested wording changes. Nothing in it changes
the live messages until Sammy decides.

What follows is what the published research and the industry say, how
solid each source is, and what it suggests for Round One.

## 1. Ex-members are a large, real source of joins

**At big chains, roughly a fifth to a third of joins are people coming
back.** Planet Fitness (US) told investors that "mid-30%" of its joins
are rejoins, **34.8% in Q4 2025**, and that it is "testing narrower
lapsed windows for rejoin offers versus waiting longer".
([Motley Fool transcript, Feb 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/24/planet-fitness-plnt-q4-2025-earnings-transcript/))
PureGym's head of CRM has said "as many as 20% of people who join
PureGym each month are re-joiners" (undated vendor case study), and a
former PureGym CFO put it at about a third in a 2024 interview.
([Braze case study](https://www.braze.com/customers/puregym-case-study),
[In Practise, March 2024](https://inpractise.com/articles/pure-gym-vs-the-gym-group))

Note what these figures measure: the share of **new joins** who are
former members, not the share of **former members** who come back. No
operator or researcher I could find publishes the second number.

**Most ex-members haven't gone to a rival.** A Dublin Institute of
Technology study (Teresa Hurley) found **85% of lapsed members had not
joined another club**, and 54% said they would consider rejoining with an
incentive. The same piece notes that people who leave a club tend to stop
exercising altogether. It is from 2014 and gives no sample size.
([Health Club Management via Fit Tech, 2014](https://www.fittechglobal.com/fit-tech-features/Everyones-talking-about-Engaging-ex-members/29177))

**The list grows quickly.** The Health & Fitness Association's 2025
benchmarking report (175 companies, 17,000+ facilities, 2024 results) put
average member retention at **66.4%**, so roughly one member in three
leaves each year. UK data from Dr Paul Bedford (2015, citing the 2013
White Report) found only 51.9% of members kept their membership for
twelve months.
([HFA](https://www.healthandfitness.org/hfa-releases-2025-fitness-industry-benchmarking-report/),
[Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Research-round-up-Member-insight/29554))

So a gym Round One's size will have more ex-members than members within
a few years, and most of them are not training anywhere.

## 2. Who is worth contacting: why they left

**The best evidence on reasons comes from outside fitness.** Kumar,
Bhagwat and Zhang (*Journal of Marketing*, 2015) studied lost customers
of a large US telecoms firm from 2006 to 2014, including a randomised
field experiment with win-back offers. They found:

- People who left **for price** were more likely to come back than those
  who left **over poor service**, and stayed longer once back.
- People who left for **both** price and service were the least likely
  to return, left again fastest and spent least.
- A **strong first relationship** predicted return: customers who had
  referred others, or whose complaints had been put right, were more
  likely to come back. Frequent complainers were less likely.

([ASU Center for Services Leadership summary](https://csl.wpcarey.asu.edu/what-you-need-know-about-customer-win-back),
[ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/07/150707134215.htm),
[paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jm.14.0107))

**Why people say they leave gyms.** YouGov's US data (June 2024) on
former gym-goers: too expensive 41%, change in personal circumstances
25%, no time 23%, moved 19%, could reach goals alone 19%, disliked the
gym experience 14%, didn't get results 5%.
([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/49804-us-why-do-consumers-turn-their-backs-on-gym-memberships))

**Why they actually leave is often different.** Bedford's UK research
found "moved house/job" given by 32% of leavers, far above the roughly 5%
of people who move each year, and money by 16%. The actual reasons, from
deeper interviews: club didn't meet expectations 17%, loss of motivation
15%, bored with the environment or classes 11%, lack of staff support
10%. No sample base is given.
([Health Club Management, 2014](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Retention-Take-your-leave/28807))

Putting these together (the mapping below is my reading; no gym study
tests return rates by reason):

| Why they left | Worth contacting? | What fits |
|---|---|---|
| Moved out of the area | No, unless they've moved back | Leave them off marketing. "Moved" is often a polite reason, so a moved-within-Bristol member is a normal prospect. |
| Cost | Yes, good odds (Kumar) | Lower commitment before lower price; see section 4 |
| Injury, illness, pregnancy | Yes, when they're ready | A personal check-in from their coach, no offer |
| Life change, no time | Yes | Timing (January, September) and class times that fit |
| Lost motivation, bored, no results | Yes, but the offer is the product, not the price | A structured restart (the 28 Day Program), a new class, a named coach |
| Bad experience or complaint | Not by mailout | A personal message from a manager, only if the problem has been put right. Kumar: least likely to return. |

Round One has some of this already: replies to the day-after-notice
win-back land on the Cancellations screen and in the weekly report
(improvement list items 7 and 12).

## 3. When: time since leaving

**There is no published gym study of return rates by months since
leaving.** I looked. What exists:

- **Vendor rules of thumb, all unsourced.** One ActiveCampaign-focused
  guide gives returns of 10–20% for people lapsed 30–90 days, 5–10% at
  90–180 days and 2–5% after 180 days.
  ([Shyn Media](https://www.shynmedia.com/activecampaign-reviews/gym-member-win-back-campaigns/))
  Nutripy: 0–30 days "is not the time to sell", 30–90 days is "the real
  reactivation window", then two or three low-pressure check-ins a year,
  with odds falling sharply after 18–24 months.
  ([Nutripy](https://nutripy.io/blog/win-back-cancelled-gym-members))
  Clubwise suggests contact at 30, 60 and 90 days after cancelling.
  ([Clubwise](https://www.clubwise.com/clubwise-blog/re-engaging-the-sleeper-fixing-frozen-gym-memberships/))
- **Operators act as if sooner is better.** Planet Fitness's "narrower
  lapsed windows" test (above) is the clearest sign.
- **An analogy from apps, not gyms.** RevenueCat's 2026 report (115,000+
  subscription apps) found about 20% of monthly subscribers come back
  within a year, against 5% of annual ones.
  ([PPC Land on RevenueCat](https://ppc.land/lapsed/))

**When people leave matters for when to ask them back.** Bedford's UK
data shows quitting peaks in July (30%) and August (24%), then November
and December (14% each).
([Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Research-round-up-Member-insight/29554))
That fits the common practice of asking people back in January and
September, when routines restart. One US martial arts operator contacts
every ex-member three times a year (January/February, May/June,
September/October). This is practice, not evidence.
([PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/how-to-run-a-reactivation-campaign-for-former-gym-members))

**The legal basis also weakens with time** (section 6), and old email
addresses bounce more (section 5).

A reasonable reading, with thin evidence behind the exact cut-offs:

| Time since membership ended | Approach |
|---|---|
| Under a month | Leave it. They've just had the notice-day win-back. |
| 1–3 months | Best odds. Personal contact for good prospects; a mailout for the rest. |
| 3–12 months | Still worth it. One or two mailouts a year, timed to January or September. |
| 12–24 months | Lower odds, higher bounce risk. At most one a year. |
| Over 24 months | Probably stop marketing (legal expectation and bounces). |

## 4. What works

### Channel

**No controlled study compares phone, email and messaging for gym
win-back.** What there is:

- **Phone converts best, by practitioner account.** A US martial arts
  chain reports calls as its "highest-converting reactivation channel",
  with no figures. ([PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/how-to-run-a-reactivation-campaign-for-former-gym-members))
- **But what the call is for matters.** In Bedford's interviews one
  leaver said: "Someone called me once when I quit to ask why I'd
  cancelled my direct debit", and it made them "feel even less valued".
  ([Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Retention-Take-your-leave/28807))
- **Messaging is read more than email**, say vendors, with no
  independent data. ([Nutripy](https://nutripy.io/blog/win-back-cancelled-gym-members))
- **Use more than one channel because details go stale.** PureGym ran
  email and SMS together so it could reach ex-members whose email or
  number was out of date. ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/customers/puregym-case-study))
- **Plain text does at least as well as designed email.** HubSpot's own
  tests found HTML-heavy emails got fewer opens and clicks than
  plain-text ones, and even one image cut clicks. It's one company's test
  from 2015 (updated 2021), but it supports the CRM's plain-text mailouts.
  ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data))

### Content

- **Personal beats generic, but a name alone isn't personal.** Sahni,
  Wheeler and Chintagunta (*Marketing Science*, 2018) found adding the
  recipient's name to the subject line lifted opens by about 20% and cut
  unsubscribes by 17%. A 2023 replication (Defau and Zauner, *Marketing
  Letters*) found no effect from first names, possibly because people
  have got used to it.
  ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/open-name-why-personalized-email-subject-lines-work),
  [Marketing Letters](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11002-023-09701-7))
  So `{first}` is the minimum; what makes an email personal is knowing
  which classes they did, who coached them and how long they trained.
- **Ex-members say nobody noticed.** Zenoti's 2026 US survey (1,393
  current and former members) found the leading reason people drift is
  "life got busy, and no one from the gym noticed"; nearly half of lapsed
  members said their gym never reached out; and lapsed members were
  nearly three times as likely to call the outreach they did get "hit or
  miss". "Lapsed" here includes people still paying. Vendor survey.
  ([Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/en-uk/thecheckin/silent-churn-crisis-2026-gym-membership-data))

### Offers

The research is clearer here than anywhere else, and it says offers
trade more people back against how long they stay.

- **Discount plus extra won the most back, but they left soonest.** In
  Kumar's field experiment the bundled offer (discount and a free
  upgrade) reacquired the most customers, who then left again soonest and
  spent least. The service upgrade alone won the fewest back, but those
  who took it stayed longest and spent most. A discount aimed at people
  who left over price was the most profitable.
  ([ASU summary](https://csl.wpcarey.asu.edu/what-you-need-know-about-customer-win-back))
- **A low price to come back, then the normal price.** Thomas, Blattberg
  and Fox (*Journal of Marketing Research*, 2004, newspaper subscribers)
  modelled both the return and how long people stayed, and concluded the
  best strategy is "a low reacquisition price and higher prices when
  customers have been reacquired".
  ([Kellogg abstract](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2004/recapturing-lost-customers?p=1))
- **Deeper discounts bring lower-value customers.** Lewis (*JMR*, 2006,
  a newspaper and an online grocer): the deeper the joining discount, the
  lower the repeat rate; a 35% discount produced customers worth about
  half the long-term value of those who paid full price.
  ([IAOR abstract](https://www.iaorifors.com/paper/53212))
- **Free trials bring people who leave faster, but who respond to
  follow-up.** Datta, Foubert and van Heerde (*JMR*, 2015, 16,512 digital
  TV customers): free-trial customers were worth 59% less over their
  lifetime and stayed about a third as long, but responded more strongly
  to marketing contact afterwards.
  ([Tilburg](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/the-challenge-of-retaining-customers-acquired-with-free-trials/),
  [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2015-04-free-trial.html))
- **In gyms, money off draws responses.** Hurley's test: a price
  discount drew a 27% response against 3% for a gift token (response, not
  rejoining; 2014; sample not given).
  ([Fit Tech](https://www.fittechglobal.com/fit-tech-features/Everyones-talking-about-Engaging-ex-members/29177))
- **Commitment may matter more than price.** A 2015 ukactive report (600+
  UK sites) found members at gyms with a 12-month minimum stayed 11.2
  months on average, against 17.2 months where contracts were flexible.
  Old data, and about joining rather than rejoining.
  ([Leisure Opportunities](https://www.leisureopportunities.co.uk/news/Flexible-gym-contracts-better-for-retaining-members:-ukactive-report/317721))

The usual gym offers, read against that:

| Offer | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| No joining fee to rejoin | Removes a barrier without cutting the monthly price; doesn't reset what the membership is worth | Only an offer if there normally is a joining fee |
| A free class or week | Easy yes; gets them back in the room | Datta: free-trial people leave faster. Needs a planned follow-up. Must be genuinely free (see below). |
| A short structured programme (the 28 Day Program) | Fits "lost motivation" and "no results" leavers; it's the service upgrade Kumar found kept people longest | Paid upfront, so a bigger ask than a free class |
| Money off the first month or two, then full price | Thomas: the right shape for a price offer; Hurley: draws responses | Lewis: deeper cuts bring people worth less; repeated, it teaches people to wait |
| Lasting discount | Wins the most back | Every source says it lowers value; current members paying full price will hear about it |

**Calling something "free" has rules.** The CAP Code (rule 3.26) says
ads "must not use the term 'free trial'" for "satisfaction or your money
back" offers or offers needing a non-refundable purchase, and the
significant conditions must sit next to the offer, not behind "T&Cs
apply".
([ASA, May 2022](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/keeping-your-free-trials-trial-free.html),
[CAP enforcement notice](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/enforcement-notice-on-free-trials-and-other-promotional-subscription-models.html))
The 28 Day Program is £79 upfront, refunded only if the person moves onto
a recurring membership, so describing it in a mailout as "free" or "a
free trial" looks risky. Describing exactly what happens to the £79 is
safe.

### How many touches, and how far apart

**One message is not enough, but the evidence on exactly how many is
practitioner advice.** Suggested cadences: three messages over about
three months (Clubwise: 30, 60, 90 days); a check-in, a reconnect four to
eight weeks later, then a low-pressure offer (Nutripy); three times a
year, every year (PushPress). PureGym cut back from emailing ex-members
"every day" on its agency's advice. The one result with numbers is about
absent members, not ex-members: in a UK club programme, over half of
absent members came back within a week of the first message and the
second message did better still (GG Fit, Crow Wood).
([Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Talking-retention/30399),
[Braze](https://www.braze.com/customers/puregym-case-study))

Consensus: two or three touches a year, spaced weeks or months apart,
not days.

## 5. What doesn't work or backfires

- **Over-discounting.** Lewis, Datta and Thomas all point the same way:
  cheap returns stay less long and are worth less. Nutripy's warning
  about blanket discounts: they train members "to cancel and wait for a
  deal". ([Nutripy](https://nutripy.io/blog/win-back-cancelled-gym-members))
- **Too many emails.** In MarketingSherpa's survey of 2,400 US consumers
  (2017) the top reasons for unsubscribing were too many emails in
  general (26%), emails not relevant (21%), too many from this company
  (19%) and "always trying to sell me something" (19%).
  ([MarketingSherpa](https://www.marketingsherpa.com/article/chart/why-consumers-unsubscribe))
- **Spam complaints and bounces have hard limits.** Gmail expects spam
  complaint rates under 0.3% (ideally under 0.1%) and requires one-click
  unsubscribe for bulk marketing.
  ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)) Resend asks for
  **bounces under 4% and spam complaints under 0.08%**, and may pause
  sending until they come down.
  ([Resend](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits))
  Email addresses go stale, so a send to people who left two years ago
  is the likeliest way for Round One to cross the bounce limit. If
  mailouts and booking emails share one Resend account, as they appear
  to, a pause would hold up booking confirmations too.
- **Guilt.** Coulter and Pinto (*Journal of Applied Psychology*, 1995)
  found moderate guilt appeals worked better than strong ones, which
  made people angry and lowered their view of the advertiser. Small
  study, but it matches Bedford's leaver who felt "even less valued"
  after a call about their direct debit.
  ([Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/guilt-appeals-in-advertising-what-are-their-effects/))
- **Generic blasts.** A vendor guide puts generic "we miss you" emails
  to all ex-members at 1–3% reactivation whatever the lapse, against more
  for segmented messages. Unsourced, but it matches every other source.
  ([Shyn Media](https://www.shynmedia.com/activecampaign-reviews/gym-member-win-back-campaigns/))
- **Marketing to people who didn't expect it.** See HelloFresh, below.

## 6. UK rules: PECR, UK GDPR and the soft opt-in

**The soft opt-in lets a business email (or text) its own customers
without separate consent**, if all of these hold: the business got the
contact details directly from the person while they bought or showed
interest in buying; the marketing is about similar products or services;
they were given a simple way to opt out **when the details were
collected**; and every message has a free, simple opt-out. The ICO
treats texts the same as email.
([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/))

**There is no fixed time limit for ex-customers.** The ICO's test is
whether people "reasonably expect direct marketing". The clearest signal
of where that runs out is the ICO's January 2024 fine of £140,000 on
HelloFresh (79 million emails and 1 million texts), partly because
customers "were not provided with sufficient information that their data
would continue to be used" for marketing for up to 24 months after
cancelling.
([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/ukico-fines-hellofresh-80-million-spam-communications))

**Fines are now much larger.** Since 5 February 2026 the Data (Use and
Access) Act 2025 lets the ICO fine PECR breaches up to £17.5m or 4% of
turnover, up from £500,000.
([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/ja/notes/data/2026-posts/uk-data-protection-and-privacy-reform-goes-live-what-you-need-to-know))

What this means in practice:

- **Opt-out at collection is the condition to check.** Most ex-members
  joined through TeamUp, not the CRM's forms. Whether TeamUp's sign-up
  (or the front desk) gave them a way to say no to marketing at the time
  decides whether the soft opt-in covers them. I couldn't check this from
  here.
- **Say how long.** Round One's privacy wording (`docs/privacy-paragraphs.md`)
  says "We may send occasional news and offers by email or WhatsApp" but
  not that this carries on after a membership ends, or for how long. The
  HelloFresh decision suggests saying so.
- **Phone calls are different.** Live marketing calls don't need
  consent, but numbers on the Telephone Preference Service can't be
  called for marketing "even if they are an existing customer", and
  anyone who has said no must go on a do-not-call list.
  ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/))
- **WhatsApp needs opt-in under Meta's own rules** as well as PECR, and
  people must be able to stop marketing messages.
  ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/overview/getting-opt-in/))
- **Already in place:** every mailout has a one-click unsubscribe and
  `List-Unsubscribe` headers, and opted-out or bounced contacts are left
  out (`docs/mailouts.md`). That covers the "opt-out in every message"
  condition and Gmail's one-click rule.

This is a summary of published guidance, not legal advice.

## 7. Benchmarks to expect

**Open rates are a poor guide.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection marks
emails as opened automatically, which inflates open rates, and every
benchmark provider measures a different sample:

| Source | Sample | Open | Click | Unsubscribe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MailerLite, Health and fitness | 3.6m campaigns, Dec 2024 to Nov 2025 | 47.81% | 1.45% | 0.3% |
| Mailchimp, all users | to Dec 2023 (no fitness row) | 35.63% | 2.62% | 0.22% |
| Benchmark Email, Health and fitness | undated | 19.85% | 0.95% | 0.09% |
| Campaign Monitor, Wellness and fitness | undated, via Rasa.io | 19.2% | 1.2% | – |

([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks),
[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/),
[Benchmark Email](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/email-marketing-benchmarks/),
[Rasa.io](https://help.rasa.io/what-are-average-open-and-click-rates-by-industry))

**Return rates: no reliable published figure.** Vendor numbers range
from 1–3% for generic blasts to 10–20% for well-targeted recent leavers,
and "5 to 10 percent" a year from a whole ex-member list; none says
where it comes from.
([Shyn Media](https://www.shynmedia.com/activecampaign-reviews/gym-member-win-back-campaigns/),
[PushPress](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/how-to-run-a-reactivation-campaign-for-former-gym-members))
Hurley's 27% was a response to a discount offer, not rejoins.

A fair expectation for a plain-text mailout: a few replies and a few
rejoins per hundred people, more from recent leavers, fewer from older
ones. The measures that matter are replies, unsubscribes, bounces and
rejoins, not opens.

## 8. During the notice period: saving people before they go

Between giving notice in TeamUp and their last day, the person is still a
member, still able to train, and their reason for leaving is fresh. What
runs today (automation "Gave notice – win-back", on): TeamUp's
`membership.cancelling` fires, the CRM waits 24 hours, then sends the
email "Before you go, {first}" (`marketing: true`) and the WhatsApp
template `win_back`. Replies land on the Cancellations screen. Staff can
put a membership on hold in TeamUp.

### Timing: notice day, a week before the end, or after it ends

**No study compares these three moments for gyms.** What there is:

- **The cancel moment is the one the industry builds around.** Clubwise
  calls the cancellation request "your last opportunity to understand
  what went wrong and potentially retain them".
  ([Clubwise](https://www.clubwise.com/clubwise-blog/re-engaging-the-sleeper-fixing-frozen-gym-memberships/))
  Subscription software firms put their save offers at that moment too
  (Churnkey, below).
- **Ignoring it costs goodwill.** Bedford's experienced exercisers
  described cancelling "with no questions asked", and felt operators
  "just don't care".
  ([Health Club Management, 2014](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Retention-Take-your-leave/28807))
- **But the decision is usually made well before notice.** The 2014
  Health Club Management piece on ex-members notes people start to
  disengage weeks or even months before they cancel
  ([Fit Tech](https://www.fittechglobal.com/fit-tech-features/Everyones-talking-about-Engaging-ex-members/29177)),
  and DellaVigna and Malmendier found a gap of over two months between
  last visit and cancelling (see `sleeping-members.md`). So a notice-day message is talking to someone
  who has mostly decided; the realistic aim is to catch the ones whose
  reason can be fixed (cost, schedule, a break), and to leave everyone
  else on good terms.
- **Pressure at the exit hurts the return.** Planet Fitness's CEO tied
  its mid-30% rejoin rate to letting members manage, and cancel, their
  memberships themselves, saying it builds goodwill.
  ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/24/planet-fitness-plnt-q4-2025-earnings-transcript/))

Reading it together: **notice day (plus 24 hours) is right for the first
message**, because the reason is fresh and there's still time to change
plan. **A week or so before the last day is the natural second touch**: a
short, practical reminder that hold and switching are still open.
**After it ends is win-back** (sections 1 to 7), not saving.

### Offers: a hold, a downgrade, or a free month

**Match the offer to the reason.** Churnkey (subscription cancel-flow
software, its own platform data) grades "reason-routed offers outperform
one-size-fits-all offers" as strong evidence: price objections go to a
smaller plan, people not using it go to a pause, and people switching to
something else get a clean exit, not a discount.
([Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/growth/library/reason-matched-save-offers))
That is the same finding as Kumar's for win-back (section 2).

**A hold beats a discount, if it has an end date.** Churnkey reports
paused subscribers resume at 54% on average (43% to 89% depending on how
the pause is set up), against 22% kept by a discount offered at the
cancel moment. It advises a resume date set when the pause starts and
shown to the person, and limits on repeat pauses. Vendor data, not
audited, and from apps and online subscriptions rather than gyms.
([Churnkey](https://churnkey.co/growth/library/pause-offer-at-cancellation))
FM Consulting (US gym consultants) say the same for gyms: freezes with a
minimum and maximum (they suggest one month to three to six months), a
fixed restart date that reactivates automatically, and no open-ended
freezes. They give no return figures.
([FM Consulting](https://www.fmconsulting.net/gym-consultant/membership-freezes-vs-cancellations-how-smart-gym-owners-save-revenue-when-members-take-breaks/))

**A downgrade keeps the relationship.** For people leaving on time or
money, a smaller membership ("right-sizing" in Churnkey's terms) keeps
them training and billing, and avoids the price cut a discount implies.
The live email already does this with Facility Access Only, Classes Only
and the student and blue-light membership.

**A free month to stay is the weakest option.** It's a discount at the
cancel moment (Churnkey's 22%), the research on discounted customers
applies (Lewis, Datta: section 4), and it teaches people that giving
notice gets a deal (Nutripy: "trains members to cancel and wait for a
deal"). If anything like it is used, it belongs after someone has left,
as a low reacquisition price (Thomas, section 4), not as a reward for
giving notice.

**Practitioner view: Gym Launch Secrets.** Alex Hormozi's *Gym Launch
Secrets* (practitioner material, US, written for owner-run gyms) treats
the exit as a conversation, not a form: an exit interview run like a
coaching or sales conversation, which a book summary says "can save half
of cancellations". That figure appears only in a second-hand summary and
I couldn't find it in the book or anywhere else; treat it as a claim. The
summary also lists regular reach-outs, attendance tracking, handwritten
cards and member events as the other retention tactics.
([SoBrief summary](https://sobrief.com/books/gym-launch-secrets),
[Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/50765829-gym-launch-secrets))
The useful part for Round One is the idea that a person, ideally the
member's coach, has the conversation; the hard-sell framing doesn't fit
the copy voice or Bedford's evidence on how leavers want to be treated.

### Tone

- **Thank them, make it easy, leave the door open.** This is what
  Planet Fitness credits for rejoins, and the opposite of what Bedford's
  leavers resented.
- **Ask, don't assume.** The reason given is often not the real one
  (Bedford, section 2). An easy question ("what's the main reason? one
  line is plenty") gets the reason that decides which offer fits.
- **No guilt, no pressure** (Coulter and Pinto, section 5).

### A second touch

The evidence that a second message helps is about absent members, not
leavers: in GG Fit's UK club programme the second message did better than
the first.
([Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Talking-retention/30399))
Practitioner advice (Clubwise, Nutripy) also favours two or three
touches. Sensible shape: one short message about a week before the last
day, only to people who haven't replied, naming the date and the two
real options (switch, or hold), then nothing until the ex-member
mailouts.

The CRM already has a `membership.ending` trigger with a days-before
count, but it fires for every membership ending, not only those who gave
notice, and automations can't yet check `contact.membership.cancelling`.
A second step would need that small addition (or a staff task instead of
a message).

### Is the notice email a service message or marketing?

**It's marketing, and `marketing: true` is right.** The ICO's guidance:
a message is a service message if it "simply gives information that they
need to know", such as "advising customers in a factual way of the
options available to them at the end of their contract", without
"encouraging or promoting one option over another". A message that
"actively promotes or encourages people to make use of a particular
service, special offer, or upgrade" is direct marketing, and the ICO's
own gym example is a payment-failure call that becomes marketing once
the gym also mentions its personal training. The PECR guide adds that
"significant promotional material aimed at getting customers ... to renew
contracts that are coming to an end" makes a message marketing, which is
the closest the ICO comes to a notice-period message. Labelling a message
"service" doesn't change it: the ICO found American Express's "service"
emails were marketing.
([ICO, identify direct marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/identify-direct-marketing/),
[ICO, PECR guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/))

The live email recommends other memberships, a coach conversation and a
no-joining-fee return. That is encouraging one option over leaving, so
it is marketing; WhatsApp's `win_back` is a marketing template for the
same reason. The consequence is that members who opted out of marketing
get nothing when they give notice. If Sammy wants them to hear
something, it would have to be a separate, strictly factual note (your
last day is X; to change your mind, reply), with no recommendations.
Check first whether TeamUp already sends a cancellation confirmation.

**The coming subscription rules allow save offers, within limits.** The
DMCC Act's subscription regime (expected spring 2027, possibly brought
forward to January 2027) requires exit "in a straightforward way and
without unnecessary hurdles"; traders "may make offers or seek feedback
during the cancellation process", but must not "frustrate or
unreasonably elongate" it.
([Browne Jacobson, April 2026](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/retail-law-roundup-april-2026/subscriptions-and-dmcc-act))
The live email sits well within that: it goes after notice is given and
asks nothing of anyone who wants to leave.

### The live email, read against all this

It already does most of what the evidence suggests: it waits until notice
is given, thanks the person, routes by reason (time or money, motivation,
something put them off), offers smaller memberships before any discount,
asks for feedback, says there's no rush and leaves the door open. The
gaps:

1. **No hold.** The WhatsApp mentions a hold; the email doesn't, and a
   hold is the option with the best evidence for people who need a
   break.
2. **The reply is open-ended.** Nothing makes replying easy for someone
   who just wants to say why.
3. **It's signed by `{team}`,** not a person.
4. **No last day.** "Until the end of your notice period" makes them
   work it out; `contact.membership.endsAt` has it when TeamUp gives one.

Suggested wording (suggestions only; the hold terms are left for Sammy to
fill in, not invented here):

> **Subject:** Before you go, {first}
>
> Hi {first},
>
> We saw you've given notice on your membership. No hard feelings at
> all, and thank you for training with us. It's been good having you in.
>
> Could you tell us the main reason? One line is plenty, just reply to
> this email. It helps us more than you'd think.
>
> And in case it helps:
>
> If you need a break (injury, work, travel, a busy few months), you can
> put your membership on hold instead of cancelling. [Hold terms: how
> long, any cost, restart date.]
>
> If it's about time or money, a different membership might suit you
> better. Facility Access Only gives you the gym floor without classes.
> Classes Only is there if the classes are what you come for. If you're a
> student or work in the blue light services, there's also a discounted
> membership with valid ID.
>
> If it's about motivation or progress, have a word with one of the
> coaches. Sometimes a different class, like Technique Thursdays, or a
> fresh plan is all it takes.
>
> If something put you off, please tell us. We'd much rather hear it than
> guess.
>
> Your membership runs until [last day], so you can keep training until
> then and there's no rush to decide. To switch, hold or stay, reply here
> or grab one of us next time you're in.
>
> And if you do leave, the door's always open. There's no joining fee if
> you want to come back later.
>
> [Name], Round One

The WhatsApp is short and already mentions the hold; the only suggested
change is the same easy question ("What's the main reason? One line is
fine.") in place of "If something put you off, tell us", if Meta's
template review allows it.

## 9. What this suggests for Round One

Suggestions, not decisions. None of these needs anything built, except
where marked.

1. **Split by how recently they left, and start with the most recent.**
   The Mailouts screen picks ex-members who ended within 3, 6, 12 or 24
   months. A first mailout to "ended within 3 months" (minus anyone who
   left in the last few weeks and has just had the notice-day win-back)
   is the smallest, warmest and lowest-risk group. Since the filter is
   "within", a later send to "within 12 months" will include the first
   group, so either word it so it reads well to both or leave a few months
   between.
2. **Stop at 24 months for marketing.** The legal expectation and the
   bounce risk both get worse with time, and the evidence that people
   come back after two years is weakest.
3. **Keep some people out of mass mailouts.** Anyone who left after a
   complaint or a bad experience (check the Cancellations replies) gets a
   personal message from a manager, or nothing. Anyone who has genuinely
   moved away gets nothing.
4. **Write it like a person, not a campaign.** Plain text, signed by a
   named person (Sammy or a coach), one clear next step (reply, or book).
   Say what's new or what might suit them. No "we miss you" on its own,
   no guilt, no mention of how long they've been away.
5. **Lead with the service, not the price.** For people who lost
   motivation or didn't see results, the 28 Day Program is the structured
   restart the research favours. For people who left on cost, lower the
   commitment before lowering the price: no joining fee, or a reduced
   first month that goes back to the normal price. Avoid lasting
   discounts, and don't run the same offer every time.
6. **Don't call the 28 Day Program "free".** Describe what happens to
   the £79 instead (CAP rule 3.26).
7. **Two or three touches a year at most per person.** January and
   September are the natural moments. A single follow-up to people who
   didn't reply is fine; a weekly sequence isn't. *(Possible addition:
   a Mailouts option to leave out anyone mailed in the last N days, using
   `mailout_recipients`.)*
8. **Call the best prospects instead of emailing them.** Recent leavers
   who trained regularly (their attendance history is on the contact),
   who referred friends, or who left because of injury or time. Ask how
   they are and what got in the way; no hard sell. Check the number
   against the TPS first.
9. **Before the first ex-member mailout:** check whether TeamUp's
   sign-up gave people a way to opt out of marketing (the soft opt-in
   condition); consider adding to the privacy wording that offers may
   continue for a set time after a membership ends; send a test; and watch
   bounces on the first send, given Resend's 4% limit.
10. **Count the results so Round One has its own numbers.** For each
    mailout, per recency group: replies (they land in Enquiries),
    unsubscribes, bounces, and rejoins within about 60 days (the nightly
    TeamUp sync shows a new membership). After two or three sends this
    will answer the time-since-leaving question better than anything
    published.

During the notice period (section 8):

11. **Keep the notice-day timing and the 24-hour wait.** The reason is
    fresh and there's still time to change plan; there's no evidence for
    waiting longer.
12. **Add the hold to the email,** with a fixed restart date and the
    terms Sammy sets (how long, any cost). It is the option with the
    best evidence for people who need a break, and the WhatsApp already
    mentions it. A hold for any member is done in TeamUp today; the
    CRM's Hold button is for unpaid members only.
13. **Ask one easy question.** "What's the main reason? One line is
    plenty." The answer decides which offer fits, and it feeds the
    Cancellations screen and weekly report (item 12).
14. **Sign it from a person, and give the last day** from `endsAt`
    instead of "the end of your notice period". Suggested full wording is
    in section 8.
15. **Offer a smaller membership or a hold, never a free month to
    stay.** Keep the no-joining-fee line for coming back later; it's a
    reacquisition offer, not a reward for giving notice.
16. **Have a coach call the people worth keeping.** Regulars and
    long-standing members who give notice get a call or a personal
    WhatsApp from their coach during the notice period: ask what's
    changed, offer the hold or a different membership, and accept the
    answer. This is the useful part of Gym Launch Secrets' exit
    interview, without the hard sell.
17. **A possible second step, about a week before the last day,** only
    to people who haven't replied: one short message naming the date and
    the two options (switch or hold). *(Needs a small addition: the
    `membership.ending` trigger can't yet be limited to people who gave
    notice. A staff task at that point is the no-build alternative.)*
18. **Keep `marketing: true` on the email and the WhatsApp.** Both
    recommend options, so under the ICO's guidance they are marketing,
    and opted-out members correctly get neither. If Sammy wants
    opted-out members told anything, it must be a strictly factual note
    (last day, how to change your mind) with no recommendations, and
    only if TeamUp doesn't already confirm the cancellation.
19. **Measure the saves.** Of those who give notice: how many reply,
    switch, hold, or stay, and how many held members restart on the
    date. Three months of that answers whether the email and any second
    step are worth it.
